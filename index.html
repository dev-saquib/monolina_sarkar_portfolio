The classroom as a

Kaizen learning laboratory.

My academic journey, shaped by a Ph.D. in Biochemistry, an M.A. in Education, and over a decade of teaching biology in higher education, informs my interest in Discipline-Based Education Research (DBER). My research explores how students develop biological understanding, connect concepts across levels of organization, and transfer knowledge to unfamiliar contexts, particularly through inquiry- and problem-based learning.

01

Critical Thinking

How students analyse scientific problems, evaluate evidence, and construct evidence-based explanations.

02

Systems Thinking

How students connect biological processes and phenomena to develop an integrated understanding of biological systems.

03

Sociocultural Perspectives

How social interactions, learning environments, and broader sociocultural contexts shape students' engagement, and reasoning.

Research Questions

Scientific Reasoning 01

Curiosity

“When AI can provide the answer, can learners retain ownership of scientific reasoning?”

I explore how learners engage with AI, focusing on whether they formulate questions, evaluate scientific evidence, test predictions, and construct evidence-based explanations rather than simply accept AI-generated answers.

Iterative Learning 02

Kaizen

“What can persistent misconceptions reveal about how learners organize knowledge—and how can it inform instructional improvement?”

I examine persistent misconceptions in higher education biology as indicators of underlying conceptual difficulties, using patterns in student responses to inform instructional improvement.

Agency, Identity & Transfer 03

Becoming

“How can biology learning environments enable students to express themselves, exercise agency, and find their place in science?”

Exploring how learning environments support learners in developing scientific confidence, exercising choice, and participating in ways that reflect their strengths, interests, and experiences.

This research vector examines pedagogical mechanisms that bridge academic biological competencies with students' personal epistemologies, fostering belonging and authentic identity formation in STEM.
