The Classroom as

a Kaizen learning laboratory.

My academic journey, shaped by a Ph.D. in Biochemistry, an M.A. in Education, and over a decade of teaching biology in higher education, informs my interest in Discipline-Based Education Research (DBER). My research explores how students develop biological understanding, connect concepts across levels of organization, and transfer knowledge to unfamiliar contexts, particularly through inquiry- and problem-based learning.

01

Critical Thinking

How students analyse scientific problems, evaluate evidence, and construct evidence-based explanations.

02

Systems Thinking

How students connect biological processes and phenomena to develop an integrated understanding of biological systems.

03

Sociocultural Perspectives

How social interactions, learning environments, and broader sociocultural contexts shape students' engagement and reasoning.

Research Questions

Scientific Reasoning 01

Curiosity

“When AI can provide the answer, can learners retain ownership of scientific reasoning?”

I explore how learners engage with AI, focusing on whether they formulate questions, evaluate scientific evidence, test predictions, and construct evidence-based explanations rather than simply accept AI-generated answers.

Iterative Learning 02

Kaizen

“What can persistent misconceptions reveal about how learners organize knowledge—and how can it inform instructional improvement?”

I examine persistent misconceptions in higher education biology as indicators of underlying conceptual difficulties, using patterns in student responses to inform instructional improvement.

Agency, Identity & Transfer 03

Becoming

“How can biology learning environments enable students to express themselves, exercise agency, and find their place in science?”

Exploring how learning environments support learners in developing scientific confidence, exercising choice, and participating in ways that reflect their strengths, interests, and experiences.

This research vector examines pedagogical mechanisms that bridge academic biological competencies with students' personal epistemologies, fostering belonging and authentic identity formation in STEM.

Practice Areas

Education Research & Collaboration 01

Education Research & Collaboration

Collaborating with academic colleagues across higher education institutions to investigate how students learn and to advance inclusive, evidence-informed approaches to scientific reasoning.

Focusing on systematic inquiry into instructional effectiveness, evidence-based curriculum redesign, and pedagogical research partnerships that bridge theory and laboratory practice.
Global Teaching & Faculty Collaboration 02

Collaborative Online International Learning

Engaging with international virtual exchange initiatives through the Global Citizenship Virtual Exchange Consortium (GCVEX), associated with Rutgers University's Graduate School of Education, and COIL Connect, New York, Brooklyn.

I am interested in how cross-context collaboration can inform pedagogical practice, enrich global learning, strengthen critical thinking, and create opportunities for collaborative education research.
Adaptability, Reusability & Knowledge Sharing 03

Open Educational Resources

Contributing educational resources and reviewing learning materials for MERLOT, supporting the discoverability, quality, and wider use of open educational resources to enhance teaching and learning.

Open educational resources offer opportunities to make quality learning materials more accessible, adaptable, and reusable. This work supports knowledge sharing and the broader educational community.
Extending Science Beyond the Classroom 04

Public Science Writing

Communicating scientific research and ideas to broader audiences through science writing, with contributions to the American Society for Microbiology (ASM) and CSIR Science Reporter.

This work focuses on translating scientific concepts into accessible narratives that connect research with wider public understanding, encourage curiosity, and make scientific knowledge more approachable for diverse audiences.
