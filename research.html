Research & Curriculum Innovation

Bridging bench biochemistry and andragogical science to investigate how undergraduates develop, refine, and transfer scientific reasoning in unfamiliar biological contexts.

01 / Theoretical Foundation

The Tripartite Inquiry Model

Three guiding vectors that organize how learners interrogate knowledge, iterate through errors, and develop authentic scientific identities.

Pillar 01 · Curiosity
AI Literacy & Epistemic Agency

Critical AI Literacy & Scientific Reasoning

"When AI can provide immediate answers, what enables learners to retain authentic ownership of scientific reasoning?"

As generative AI becomes ubiquitous in higher education, biological education faces a pivotal crossroad: will students become passive consumers of synthesized explanations, or will they leverage AI as an epistemic thought partner? Our research constructs inquiry-based protocols where learners actively interrogate algorithmic outputs, identify hallucinations in biochemical pathway models, and formulate prompt-evaluation rubrics that elevate critical thinking above cognitive offloading.

Core Instructional Innovation:
Replacing traditional essay writing with "AI Output Audits," where students critique AI-generated mechanistic models of nitrogen-cycling and cellular stress response against primary research literature.
Pillar 02 · Kaizen
Conceptual Change & Iterative Feedback

The Classroom as a Continuous Kaizen Laboratory

"What can persistent misconceptions reveal about how learners organize biological knowledge—and how can data analytics iteratively optimize learning experiences?"

Biological knowledge frequently suffers from fragmented, context-independent compartmentalization. Students often memorize citric acid cycle intermediates without connecting them to thermodynamic laws, evolutionary adaptation, or environmental stress. Drawing upon Kaizen—the philosophy of deliberate, continuous improvement—we implement micro-assessments, formative concept inventories, and SPSS analytics to track conceptual change week-by-week.

Empirical Method:
Auditing high-attrition course nodes and deploying adaptive scaffolding, reducing conceptual bottlenecks in undergraduate biochemistry cohorts.
Pillar 03 · Becoming
Agency, Identity & Intercultural Transfer

Agency, Scientific Identity & Authentic Transfer

"How does learning science change what learners believe they can do—and where can they see themselves participating in science?"

Education is not a credentialing checkbox; it is a transformative state of evolving mastery. Through our collaboration with Eastern India's first women's university and virtual cross-border classrooms via Rutgers GCVEX, we investigate how authentic research encounters dismantle imposter phenomenon, cultivate belonging, and empower students from diverse demographics to see themselves as legitimate contributors to international scientific discourse.

Global Impact:
Facilitating collaborative research exchanges connecting students to global biotechnology and sustainable agriculture challenges.
02 / National Standards

DBER Consensus Alignment (NRC, 2012)

Grounded in the National Research Council's foundational report on Discipline-Based Education Research in Undergraduate Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics.

Dimension I

Epistemic & Conceptual Practices

Understanding how undergraduate learners construct, refine, and deploy mental models across disparate biological organizational tiers—from atomic bonding and macromolecular conformations to cellular signal transduction and ecosystem-scale biogeochemical cycles.

Dimension II

Nature & Development of Expertise

Scaffolding the cognitive shift from novice feature-matching (e.g., memorizing reaction arrows) to expert representational competence—interpreting spectrophotometric data, metabolomic chromatograms, and genomic variant trees with biological fluency.

Dimension III

Validated Objectives & Interventions

Measuring instructional innovations through rigorous classroom action research, using SPSS statistical modeling, validated concept inventories, Universal Design for Learning (UDL) rubrics, and longitudinal learning analytics to assure reproducible efficacy.
